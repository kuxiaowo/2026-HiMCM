Abstract

The rebound of tiger populations in Nepal over the last decade has renewed the hope for species conservation. The majority of Nepal’s tiger population lives in the Chitwan-Parsa Complex (CPC), a protected area covering both the core parks, which prohibit humans, and buffer zones in which some human activities are permitted. To better understand the distribution of the burgeoning tiger population within the CPC and its adjacent forested areas, we constructed a Bayesian occupancy model based on data from an extensive sign survey conducted by the Department of National Parks and Wildlife Conservation between December 2020 and February 2021. The average estimated occupancy within 10 km X 10 km grids and detection within 2 km X 2km subgrids were [0.90 (CI 0.76 – 0.98)] and [0.34 (CI 0.32 -0.36)], respectively. Tigers were found to be more strongly related to prey occupancy at the home-range scale, whereas factors such as vegetation, human population density, and elevation affected which portion of the home range was used. Human population density significantly reduced habitat use by tigers and prey. We compare our results against “tiger rescue” records, where veterinary teams track and relocate specific tigers involved in conflicts. We found that in the buffer region, 2.8 times more rescues occurred in subgrids with above-average estimated use. The high number of tiger rescue cases within high-use buffer regions indicates an increased likelihood of human-tiger conflict in those areas and could inform future approaches to tiger conservation and conflict resolution.

Description of the data and file structure

The rebound of tiger populations in Nepal over the last decade has renewed the hope for species conservation. The majority of Nepal’s tiger population lives in the Chitwan-Parsa Complex (CPC), a protected area covering both the core parks, which prohibit humans, and buffer zones in which some human activities are permitted.

We compare our results against “tiger rescue” records, where veterinary teams track and relocate specific tigers involved in conflicts. We found that in the buffer region, 2.8 times more rescues occurred in subgrids with above-average estimated use.

The high number of tiger rescue cases within high-use buffer regions indicates an increased likelihood of human-tiger conflict in those areas and could inform future approaches to tiger conservation and conflict resolution. This dataset contains raw data used in analysis in .csv format and .R software (R) files for data analysis processing. Any empty cells represent missing data.

Data was derived from the following sources:

  • Field data- Data.csv: Data for occupancy analysis. This data file contains the following variables used in occupancy analysis:

    Rep- replicate number; Location- Location of sampling points; Date- Date of data recorded; Featured- Geographical features like fireline or road or animal trails; Terrain- Terai or Siwaliks; Habitat type- Types of habitat where animals were reported; GPS location- Latitude and Longitude; Animals names- Tiger, Leopard, Other carnivores, Elephant, Spotted deer, Wild boar, Sambar, Barking deer, Blue bull, gaur; Other1- other animals reported during the study; Remarks- additional information; Grid2_2- Grid size of 2x2 km and; Grid10 10- Grid size of 10x10 km.

  • pred-prey_data_csv_(version_1).csv: Data used to revise occupancy analysis.

    Year: The year when the data was recorded.; National_park: The protected area where the survey was done (like Parsa, Chitwan, etc.).Prey_density (per sq km): How many prey animals (like deer, wild boar, etc.) are found per square kilometer in that park.; Tiger_density (per sq km): Number of tigers living per square kilometer.; Leopard_density (per sq km): Number of leopards living per square kilometer.; Prey_biomass (kg/sq km): Total estimated weight of all prey animals per square kilometer (how much food is available for predators).; Tiger_biomass (kg/sq km): Total weight of tigers per square kilometer (combined body mass of tigers in that area).; Leopard_biomass (kg/sq km): Total weight of leopards per square kilometer.; Predator_biomass (kg/sq km): Combined total weight of all predators (tigers + leopards) per square kilometer.

  • Wild_tiger_and_leopard_capture_siteR5.csv: Data for rescue data updates.
  • Wild_tiger_and_leopard_capture_siteR4.csv: Data for rescue data updates.
Code/Software

Files are stored in R software (.R) format. RStudio (Software capable of running R) is used for statistical analysis and data visualization.

Occupancy_1-24_coefficients.R: Defines model coefficients used in the occupancy analysis.

Occupancy_1-24_data_preparation.R: Prepares and processes data, including variable selection for analysis.

Occupancy_1-24_model_only.R: Builds and runs the occupancy models used in the analysis.

Occupancy_1-24_simulation_only.R: Runs simulations based on the occupancy model framework.
